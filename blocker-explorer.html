Whole trees. Whole adversaries.
Different meanings of a face.

Take f(x,y) = x OR y. A vertex records an entire execution, ending at the first query that determines the answer. Keep those six vertices fixed and change which sets are faces.

The six ordered exits

Blue: query x first · Orange: query y first

An ordered transcript is τ = ((i₁,b₁), …, (iₖ,bₖ)), with no repeated query and length ℓ(τ) = k. Write f|τ for the restriction along it. An exit first determines the output at its final query. Here the exit set is ℰf = {a,b,c,d,e,f}.

The two complete trees have exit sets {a,b,c} and {d,e,f}. A whole tree is a triangle, not a vertex.

What does I remove?

Start with every subset. Forbid a whole tree and every set containing it.

Write 𝒯fmax = {{a,b,c}, {d,e,f}}. The independence complex is

I(𝒯fmax) = {U ⊆ ℰf : {a,b,c} ⊈ U and {d,e,f} ⊈ U}.

The tree sets are maximal allowed faces in 𝒯f. They become minimal forbidden faces in I(𝒯fmax): each is excluded, but all its proper subsets are allowed.

There are 64 subsets to start with, including the empty set. Exclude the 8 sets containing {a,b,c} and the 8 containing {d,e,f}; their overlap is the full six-element set. This leaves 49 faces including the empty face.

Remove the facets from 𝒯f

The two filled triangles lose their interiors. Only their edges and vertices remain. Mixed edges and triangles are still absent.

Dimension 1 · two circles

Form I(𝒯fmax)

Mixed sets are allowed. A maximal set has two vertices from each triple: four vertices form a tetrahedron. There are 3 × 3 = 9 choices.

Dimension 3 · a triangulated S³

The nine tetrahedra are glued according to their shared vertex sets. Together they form the join of two triangle boundaries, ∂Δ{a,b,c} * ∂Δ{d,e,f}. This is a 3-sphere. The explorer shows each tetrahedron in ordinary 3D; it does not claim to embed the whole 3-sphere in 3D.

The same exits, four different questions

𝒯f is the complex of partial trees; 𝒜f is the complex of partial adversary strategies. A partial tree S ∈ 𝒯f is represented by its selected exits.

𝒯fmax := Facets(𝒯f),   𝒜fmax := Facets(𝒜f).

Here “max” means inclusion-maximal. Write T ∈ 𝒯fmax for a complete tree and A ∈ 𝒜fmax for a complete adversary.

𝒯fmax and 𝒜fmax are a clutter and its blocker. The two independence complexes are Alexander dual on the exit set ℰf. 𝒯f and 𝒜f are not, in general, that Alexander-dual pair.

For a budget k, let Lk = {τ ∈ ℰf : ℓ(τ) ≤ k} be the exits of length at most k. Then D(f) > k exactly when Lk is a face of I(𝒯fmax). For OR on two bits, L1 = {c,f}; adversary {a,d} avoids both, forcing two queries.

The example is an exact finite illustration. It does not establish a new general query-complexity lower bound.
