Why this contraction works

f(x₁,x₂,x₃) = x₃ if x₁ = 0; x₂ if x₁ = 1.

This is the selector used in the original explorer. At 1−, the output only depends on x₃, so x₂ is irrelevant. We contract 1−–2−. At 1+, the output only depends on x₂, so x₃ is irrelevant. We then contract 1+–3−.

The first part of the animation only changes the drawing: it places 1− and 1+ at subdivision points of a tetrahedron. Each contraction then slides one subdivision point to an endpoint. Two triangles flatten away at each completed contraction, one from each output region. The resulting regions are still two disks sharing a circle.

The inverse is visible: move the slider backward to reinsert the subdivision points. This simultaneously recovers the original triangulations of B⁰, B¹, and X. The new opposite-literal edges represent old paths; they are not new consistent assignments.

Scope and mathematical checks

The example is nonmonotone and is not a graph property. Its two output regions and ambiguity circle are enumerated directly from its eight inputs. At each endpoint we form the simplicial image, check the intersection face by face, and verify that stellar subdivision restores all three previous complexes. This illustrates the local irrelevant-coordinate contraction; arbitrary collections of irrelevant edges cannot automatically be contracted safely.
